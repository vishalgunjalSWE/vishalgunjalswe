### 🧠 Engineering Philosophy (Borrowed from Google SRE)

| Principle | What It Means | How I Apply It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Everything Fails** | Design for failure, not success. | Multi-AZ deployments, circuit breakers, graceful degradation. |
| **Toil is the Enemy** | Automate repetitive work. | GitOps, drift detection, self-healing infrastructure. |
| **Observability ≠ Monitoring** | Understand the unknowns. | Distributed tracing, correlation IDs, SLIs/SLOs. |
| **Security by Default** | Zero trust architecture. | RBAC, Network Policies, no hardcoded secrets (Vault/SOPS). |
| **Error Budgets** | Balance velocity and reliability. | SLO tracking, controlled risk, deployment freezes. |

### 🌟 What Sets Me Apart

**1. I Think in Systems, Not Tools**  
> *Most engineers:* "I know Docker, Kubernetes, and Terraform."  
> **Me:** "I understand distributed systems failure modes. I use Kubernetes for declarative state reconciliation and self-healing, not because it's trendy."

**2. I Design for Failure**  
> *Most engineers:* "My app works in testing."  
> **Me:** "I've tested the blast radius: What happens when RabbitMQ goes down? (DLQ prevents message loss). What if AWS loses an AZ? (Multi-AZ with auto-failover)."

**3. I Document Decisions (RFCs & ADRs)**  
> *Most engineers:* "I built it."  
> **Me:** "I documented *why* I chose RabbitMQ over Kafka, mapped the architecture, wrote the runbooks, and logged the post-mortem of what I learned when it broke."
